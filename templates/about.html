About This Project

Scientific background, computational survival methodology, multi-omics architecture, and explainable AI framework.

EDUCATIONAL DEMONSTRATION NOTICE: This web application and all associated predictive models have been constructed exclusively for academic benchmarking, research presentation, and algorithmic demonstration. The underlying dataset is synthetically generated and must NOT be utilized for clinical diagnosis, patient management, or therapeutic guidance.
1. Problem Statement: The Challenge of Cancer Survival Modeling

Colorectal adenocarcinoma (COAD) remains one of the leading causes of cancer-related mortality worldwide. Accurate patient prognosis is critical for personalized oncology, adjuvant chemotherapy stratification, and clinical trial enrollment. However, classical statistical modeling in oncology faces substantial hurdles:

Extreme Tumor Heterogeneity

Patients diagnosed with identical histological subtypes and AJCC stages frequently experience drastically divergent clinical outcomes due to underlying molecular variation.

Right-Censored Lifetime Data

Clinical endpoints are incomplete: patients drop out of trials, reach study conclusion alive, or are lost to follow-up, invalidating standard regression and classification algorithms.

2. Research Objectives

This project establishes a modern computational prototype designed to address the aforementioned challenges through three foundational aims:

Multi-Omics Fusion

Seamlessly combine clinical staging, transcriptomic gene expression, and epigenetic DNA methylation biomarkers into a unified feature space.

Non-Linear Survival Analysis

Benchmark conventional semi-parametric Cox models against non-linear Random Survival Forests capable of handling complex gene-gene interactions.

Actionable XAI Interpretability

Deliver transparent Shapley attributions for bedside transparency, transforming black-box ensemble decisions into interpretable biological insights.

4. Survival Machine Learning Methodology
Cox Proportional Hazards (CPH)

A semi-parametric model formulated as h(t|X) = h₀(t) • exp(βᵀX). It models the hazard ratio as a linear combination of covariates. While computationally stable and readily interpretable, CPH assumes proportional hazards over time and struggles to capture high-order non-linear biomarker interactions.

Random Survival Forests (RSF)

An ensemble tree-based method extending Breiman's random forests to right-censored outcomes using log-rank splitting criteria. RSF computes cumulative hazard functions by aggregating across individual survival trees, naturally discovering non-linear interactions without proportional hazard restrictions.

5. Explainable AI (SHAP Methodology)

Clinical deployment of artificial intelligence requires total auditability. To prevent uninterpretable predictions, we utilize SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations):

  • TreeSHAP Algorithm: Employs exact polynomial-time computation to trace conditional expectation paths across decision trees in the survival forest.
  • Additive Risk Attribution: Translates abstract ensemble hazard functions into patient-specific additive contributions, where red bars depict elevated risk factors and green bars denote protective biomarkers.
  • Global Permutation & Feature Importance: Aggregates absolute Shapley values across the test cohort to rank the global predictive authority of each feature.
6. Survival Model Evaluation: The C-Index

Standard classification metrics (Accuracy, ROC-AUC, F1-score) are unsuitable for survival analysis due to variable follow-up durations and censoring. Instead, survival models are evaluated with Harrell's Concordance Index (C-Index):

Harrell's C-Index Formula: Proportion of all evaluable patient pairs where the model correctly predicts who experiences the event first.
  • C = 0.5: Equivalent to a random coin flip.
  • 0.7 ≤ C ≤ 0.8: Strong discrimination in clinical biomarker modeling.
  • C = 1.0: Perfect discrimination of relative event timings.
7. Methodological Limitations
  • Synthetic Benchmark Data: Features and events are simulated from parameterized Gaussian copulas and synthetic hazard rates. They do not mirror real biological tumor phenotypes.
  • Limited Cohort Dimensionality: For educational clarity, the dataset is restricted to 3 gene expression and 2 methylation markers rather than full genome-wide ~20,000 gene panels and ~450k CpG sites.
  • No Prospective Multi-Center Validation: Models have not undergone prospective clinical trials, external hospital validation, or regulatory clearance (e.g. FDA SaMD).
