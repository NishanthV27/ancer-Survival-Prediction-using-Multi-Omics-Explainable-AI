The Kaplan-Meier Estimator

The Kaplan-Meier (KM) estimator is a non-parametric statistic used to estimate the survival function S(t) from lifetime data. The probability of surviving past time t is computed as the cumulative product of conditional probabilities:

S(t) = ∏ti ≤ t [ 1 − (di / ni) ]
  • • ti: Distinct observed event time points
  • • di: Number of observed endpoint events (mortality) at time ti
  • • ni: Number of individuals at risk immediately prior to time ti
Right Censoring in Oncology

Survival analysis uniquely handles censored observations — situations where the exact time of the event is unobserved. In clinical follow-up cohorts, right censoring occurs when:

  • Study Termination: Patient remains alive and healthy at the conclusion of the trial period.
  • Loss to Follow-up: Patient moves away, changes care centers, or discontinues communication.
  • Competing Risks: Patient dies from causes unrelated to cancer (e.g., accidental trauma).
Censored individuals contribute risk time up to their last verified contact without introducing survival bias.
